🌟 What is the Gaia G band?
The Gaia G band is a wide optical filter covering roughly
330–1050 nm

It includes:

blue light

visible light

part of the near-infrared

Gaia uses it for astrometry and photometry of all sources

It is not the same as Johnson V, Sloan g, or TESS magnitudes.

📏 What does “G-band magnitude” mean?
A G-band magnitude is:

A logarithmic measure of how bright a star appears in Gaia’s broad optical band as observed from Earth.

Key properties:

Lower value → brighter star

Higher value → fainter star

Logarithmic scale:

difference of 5 mag = factor of 100 in brightness

Example:

G = 10 → very bright

G = 15 → moderately faint

G = 20 → very faint (near Gaia’s limit)

extinction

Distance-corrected (via Gaia models)

“How bright the star would appear at 10 parsecs”

This allows physical comparison between stars.

🧠 Why Gaia uses the G band
Very high signal-to-noise

Uniform across the entire sky

Optimised for:

astrometry

stellar classification

distance estimation

Works for both:

faint stars
